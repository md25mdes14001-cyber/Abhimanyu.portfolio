The Problem

The 55-Point Gap — a relational capacity crisis

Young adults want meaningful partnership but lack the skills, confidence, and opportunities to pursue it. 1 in 2 young adults feel lonely regularly (UCLA Loneliness Scale). 5+ hours daily on screens, not with people. 30% decline in social interactions since 2003 (American Trends Panel). 63% of men under 30 are single (US).

Meanwhile, the AI companion market is exploding — 10M+ users globally, projected $12.5B by 2032. The risk: AI companions that replace human connection rather than build relational capacity toward it.

"Technology should build bridges to humans, not walls around us."

The Solution

Human-First AI Companion — optimizing for relational flourishing, not engagement

The first AI companion designed to make itself unnecessary. We optimize for relational flourishing, not engagement. Three core design interventions address the relational capacity crisis systematically.

01 · Micro-Date Protocol
AI → Practice → Human
Graduated practice before real dates. AI coaching → 3 practice modes → human match. Build confidence through safe practice. The AI becomes a bridge, not a destination.
02 · Relational Health Hours
2-Hour Daily AI Limit
Human interaction credits and RHH dashboard tracking. Encourage real-world connection by design. The system measures and rewards offline social time.
03 · Deliberate Friction
AI Disagrees. Slower Responses.
Designed exit at Day 90. Build resilience and independence. The AI is designed to push users toward humans — not pull them toward itself.
Longitudinal Pilot
3-Year Study · 500 Participants
RCT Design. Measure: UCLA Scale, Social Network Index, Confidence Rating. Track: Human Connection Score (HCS). Goal: 40%+ improvement from baseline. Final assessment at 36 months.
