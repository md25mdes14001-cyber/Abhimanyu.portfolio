My Approach

Systems thinking
meets behavioral craft.

01
The visible problem is downstream.

I start every project by asking: what is the actual system generating this behavior? Most designers address symptoms. I try to address the underlying architecture — behavioral, social, or systemic — that produces them.

02
Design for behavior, not features.

A feature that doesn't change behavior is decoration. Every design decision I make is evaluated against one question: does this make the right action easier, more visible, or more legible than the alternative?

03
Coherence over optimization.

Engagement metrics measure habit formation in the product, not actual outcomes. I design for coherence — systems that leave people genuinely better off, not just more engaged.

04
Don't design in a vacuum.

Great design emerges from deep fieldwork. Cultural context, behavioral psychology, and lived experience are the material I work with.

My design process

1
Understand & align

I don't just ask questions — I ask the right ones. I dig into behavioral psychology, cultural context, business constraints, and system dynamics to uncover what's actually generating the problem. If a brief is murky, I clarify it. That's where real design starts.

2
Design & collaborate

I move from system maps to interaction models with speed and intention. Every screen is grounded in behavioral insight and practical constraints. I annotate clearly, structure files for handoff, and believe collaboration is honest — not just a checkbox.

3
Test & adapt

My work doesn't stop at handoff. I validate through research, prototyping, and post-launch iteration. I'm not afraid to change direction when evidence demands it — and I make sure insights feed directly into the next round.
